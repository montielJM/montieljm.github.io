Research

Duplicate Medical Records in Emergency Care

This study investigates how duplicate medical records affect emergency clinicians' information seeking, decision-making, and workflow within electronic health record systems.

Overview

Electronic health records are intended to provide clinicians with accurate and accessible patient information. However, duplicate medical records can create fragmented information environments that complicate clinical work and healthcare decision-making.

My dissertation examines how duplicate medical records function as sociotechnical conditions that shape disturbances in clinical work practices.

Research Objectives

  • To explore clinicians' cognitive load, emotions, and information behaviors as they interact with duplicate medical records during emergency care.
  • To identify and characterize underlying tensions revealed through clinicians' interactions with duplicate medical records during emergency care.

Conceptual Mechanisms

Activity Theory provides the conceptual lens for examining interactions among clinicians, electronic health records, organizational workflows, and duplicate patient information.

The framework supports understanding how clinicians navigate tensions, adapt information behavior, and make decisions within complex healthcare environments.

Methods

This study employs a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design to investigate clinician information behavior when interacting with duplicate medical records.

Phase 1: Quantitative

  • Survey of emergency clinicians
  • Analysis of relationships among cognitive, emotional, and behavioral dimensions.

Phase 2: Qualitative

  • Semi-structured interviews
  • Thematic analysis
  • Identification of tensions and adaptive behavior.

Expected Contributions

This research examines how individuals interact with information in medical records and digital health systems, such as EHRs, to inform the design of technologies that better support patient care.
